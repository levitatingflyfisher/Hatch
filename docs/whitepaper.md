# Hatch — the strategic case

## The gap

Children's math apps split into two failures. The *engagement-first* shelf
(the top of every app store) wraps arithmetic in an unrelated game — the
"chocolate-covered broccoli" the learning-sciences literature has documented
for thirty years — and monetizes children with the darkest patterns in
software: membership nagging, locked-content teasing, energy timers, ads
styled as gameplay. Audits of the five most-downloaded preschool games found
aesthetic-manipulation and collection-compulsion patterns in *all five*. The
*pedagogy-first* shelf (mastery systems in the Math Academy lineage) gets the
science right — mastery gating, spaced retrieval, interleaving — but is built
for motivated older students and priced as a subscription service.

Nothing on either shelf is: correct about the science, genuinely game-shaped,
free, private, and safe to hand a seven-year-old unsupervised.

## The bet

A structurally trustworthy fact-fluency game: the practitioner-consensus
curriculum (derived-fact strategies, CPA weaning) scheduled by a mastery
engine (calendar-day spaced retrieval, placement, interleaving), inside a game
whose verbs are the mathematics itself — built by a family-tools workshop
whose privacy guarantees are architectural. The APK requests zero permissions;
there is no server, no account, no ad, no purchase, and no way to add one
without failing the build.

The design's north star is the intrinsic-integration result: children given
free choice played the math-in-the-mechanic version of the same game seven
times longer than the math-as-quiz version — and learned more per minute.
Hatch's egg tray is that idea taken literally: an incubator tray of eggs *is*
an array, so the game and the mathematics are the same object — and when a
fact turns automatic it hatches into a critter the child keeps.

## Who it serves

A household: a seven-year-old learning her tables, her older sister who half
knows them (placement finds each child's real frontier in one session), a
pre-reader sibling (numerals-only play path), and a parent who wants a
five-second answer to "how is she doing?" (the Album doubles as the
heatmap) without anyone's data leaving the device.

## Why free and open

The one-sentence economics: this is a gift, not a funnel. MIT-licensed, local
-first, F-Droid-eligible. The engine (`mastery_core`) is deliberately
topic-generic — division is the same trays asked backwards; addition facts
are a simpler strip toy — so the marginal cost of the next course is a content
pack, not an app.
