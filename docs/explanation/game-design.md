# The game design

Why Hatch plays the way it does — each choice traces to the research brief
and survived an adversarial design review.

## The math is the mechanic

The controlling result (Habgood & Ainsworth; replicated with an attentional
mechanism in a 2022 pre-registered study): learning content must be embodied
in the core mechanic, delivered through the fun part — because players
process only what the game task *requires*. Kids given free choice played the
integrated version of the same game 7× longer and remembered more.

So in Hatch there is no math bolted between fun bits. An incubator tray *is*
an a×b array; you cannot fill a 7×8 tray from your own mastered trays without
decomposing 7 or 8; a 2×5 tray will not fit a 5×2 slot until you rotate it —
and that rotation *is* the commutativity lesson. Swap the multiplication out
and every verb (stack, fold, slice, snap, rotate) becomes meaningless — the
Trash-Zapper test, passed structurally.

## Misses teach; the error is the lesson

A wrong answer renders as its actual quantity against the true frame: 24 eggs
in a 4×7 tray leaves four wells blinking while a ghost row slides in — "one
more four" — and 32 spills two eggs off the edge. That animation *is* the
±one-group derivation, shown at the moment of maximum attention. The fact
re-asks before the round ends (re-retrieval closes the debt; engine law 8).
The miss sound is the quietest in the app.

## The three surfaces

- **Nursery** (Baroody phases 1–2): build trays; derived-fact strategies are
  one-tap seam moves taught by a wordless ghost-cursor lesson using the
  child's *own* mastered trays. Choice between routes where the math offers
  two (×6 = double ×3, or ×5 plus a row) — autonomy inside the curriculum.
- **Hatch Rush** (phase 3): the arcade layer, where lifetime reps live.
  Sub-minute, instant restart, racing your own ghost caterpillar — never a
  clock, never another child. Flappy Bird's loop shape with the compulsion
  mechanics amputated: the chase is your own yesterday.
- **Album & Habitats** (the meta): every fact is a critter that hatches at
  true automaticity. The album is fully visible from minute one — no
  silhouettes, no locks-as-treasure; unhatched facts are just eggs, honestly
  waiting. Habitats are the unscored shelf the child arranges herself.

## Juice, and where it's aimed

Squash-and-stretch, particles, the row-by-row crack cascade, the 1.2-second
hatch moment: all feedback on the child's *own action*, never on a reward
schedule. That's the ethically clean half of game feel (Juice it or Lose it,
minus the overclaim — juice supports a loop, it can't replace one).

## What Hatch refuses, permanently

Streaks and daily anything; energy and appointment mechanics; locked-content
teasing; leaderboards and sibling comparison; ads and purchases (structurally
impossible — zero permissions); visible timers and public speed; negative
feedback of any kind. The engagement claim is calibrated accordingly: Hatch
aims to be the ten-minute dose she never resists and sometimes picks up
unprompted — the fight it refuses to win is the one that would require the
weapons above.
